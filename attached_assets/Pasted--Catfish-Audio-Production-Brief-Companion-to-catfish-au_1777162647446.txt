# Catfish Audio — Production Brief

Companion to `catfish_audio_assets.csv` (65 audio assets total).

This brief covers the *strategy* — what to build, in what order, how
they fit together, and the integration points with the existing engine
and Date Mode PRD.

## Why these specific tracks (the design logic)

### The eight hero tracks form a complete app-state map

Every screen the player can be on has a music bed:

|Player is in…                                |Plays                   |
|---------------------------------------------|------------------------|
|Title / menus                                |`main_theme`            |
|Swipe deck                                   |`swipe_mode_loop`       |
|Active chat thread                           |`chat_mode_bed`         |
|Date preamble (between accept and scene load)|`date_mode_anticipation`|
|Date scene, things going well                |`date_mode_loop_warm`   |
|Date scene, focus shift / tense moment       |`date_mode_loop_tense`  |
|Journal review                               |`journal_theme`         |
|Accusation modal                             |`accusation_theme`      |

The transitions between these are scripted moments — `main_theme` →
`swipe_mode_loop` is the “I’m starting a new run” feeling. `chat_mode_bed`
→ `date_mode_anticipation` is the “this is happening now” feeling. The
music itself becomes a UX cue.

### date_mode_loop_warm ↔ date_mode_loop_tense crossfade is the most

### important musical moment in the game

These two tracks should share the same instrumentation and key center but
differ in mode (major vs. minor) and one held-note presence. The
crossfade between them is the *emotional* version of the Focus Shift —
same surface, different reality underneath. Players will subconsciously
associate the tense bed with “they’re hiding something” by the second
date. By the fourth date, hearing it cross in becomes a foreshadowing
cue.

If you only get to compose perfectly TWO things in this OST, make it
these two.

### The endings each subvert main_theme differently

Notice the ending tracks all reference `main_theme` melodically but
distort it in different ways:

- **caught_them**: slower and contemplative — bittersweet
- **wrongful_accusation**: quoting it pitched-down — wrong
- **met_killer**: detuned, distant — lost
- **killer_escaped**: continuing it as if nothing happened — bleak

This means players who replay will recognize the main theme echoed in
each ending, which makes the credits roll (which uses `main_theme` again)
hit harder. Free emotional ROI from compositional discipline.

## Audio mixing rules

These belong codified in `VoicePlayer` (Date Mode PRD Epic 2.4) and the
broader audio engine:

### Volume hierarchy (loudest → quietest)

1. **Voice (ElevenLabs scripted dialogue, on-demand)** — full level
1. **Stings** — peak at -6dB relative to voice
1. **UI SFX** — peak at -10dB relative to voice
1. **Music** — sits at -15dB during voice, -8dB without voice (auto-duck)
1. **Diegetic ambience** — sits at -25dB always, -30dB during voice

Music ducks when voice plays. Ambience ducks slightly further so it
stays atmospheric without competing. UI SFX should be just loud enough
to feel responsive but never compete with voice.

### Implementation: AVAudioEngine submix routing

Three audio buses, each a separate `AVAudioMixerNode`:

- **Music bus** — long loops, ducked dynamically
- **SFX bus** — UI + stings + diegetic one-shots
- **Voice bus** — ElevenLabs scripted + on-demand

The director sends a “voice playing” notification that the music bus
listens to and ducks accordingly. This is standard AVFoundation work,
your wheelhouse.

### Audio session category

`.playback` with `.mixWithOthers` so the player can have Spotify running.
For the Date Mode dialogue specifically, the app should *interrupt* their
music — set `.playback` without `.mixWithOthers` only during date scenes,
restore on scene exit.

## Production order recommendation

### Phase 1 (audio MVP — alongside engine Phase A)

Get something audible into the swipe + chat experience as soon as
possible. Music has a longer tail than visuals — players will tolerate
placeholder music longer than they will silence.

1. **`main_theme`** — compose this first. It’s the OST anchor, every other
   composition references it.
1. **`swipe_mode_loop`** — second compose priority. Most-heard track.
1. **All 22 UI SFX** — generate via ElevenLabs in one batch. Cheap,
   immediate game-feel improvement.
1. **`message_send` and `message_received`** are in the UI batch — those
   two specifically make the chat feel alive.
1. **`match_celebration_sting`, `clue_discovered_sting`** — first two
   stings to generate. Already needed for swipe right and journal logging.

Result: app feels alive end-to-end. Music carries main menu and swipe.
Chat has SFX. Journal has SFX.

### Phase 2 (Date Mode PRD Phase A)

When you start building the date scene plumbing:

1. **`date_mode_anticipation`** — compose. Date preamble screen needs music.
1. **`date_mode_loop_warm`** — compose. The default date bed.
1. **`focus_shift_sting`** — generate via ElevenLabs.
1. **`dialogue_advance_tap`, `choice_button_select`** — UI SFX for date scenes.
1. **All 8 ambience loops** — generate batch. Each date environment needs
   one. Drop them in as soon as the matching environment art is in.

### Phase 3 (Date Mode PRD Phase C — killer tell mechanic)

1. **`date_mode_loop_tense`** — compose. The half of the warm/tense
   crossfade that makes the Focus Shift land.
1. **`saint_mask_subliminal_tone`, `saint_mask_first_glimpse`,
   `clue_discovered_date_subtle`** — generate. The Saint Mask layer’s
   audio half.
1. **Diegetic one-shots** — generate batch. Lower priority but
   substantially raises immersion in dates.

### Phase 4 (polish + endings)

1. **Four ending tracks** — compose. Last because they’re heard once per
   ending, and you’ll have the most musical context to draw on after
   composing the other 8 hero tracks.
1. **`accusation_theme`** — compose.
1. **`journal_theme`** — compose. Lowest priority hero track because the
   journal is a power-user feature.
1. **Remaining stings and narrative SFX** — generate.

## ElevenLabs music generation tips

ElevenLabs’s text-to-music endpoint is good at short clips, less reliable
on long ones. The CSV reflects this — every ElevenLabs-source track is
≤4 seconds except ambience (which is a different model concern, not
musical composition).

**For stings specifically:**

- Generate 3-4 variants of each and cherry-pick the best
- Keep prompts concrete (BPM, instrument, target emotion) rather than
  abstract
- Reference the matching hero track in the prompt where applicable
  (“complementing the warm major-key feel of the swipe deck music”)
- Specify “hard out, no fade” or “cold start” explicitly — ElevenLabs
  tends to fade-in/out by default

**For ambience:**

- The prompts are deliberately specific about volume layering (“at -25dB,”
  “barely audible”) because ambience has to be readable as background
- Don’t accept loops with obvious seams — regenerate or use
  Audacity to manually crossfade-loop
- 60 seconds is the minimum for a non-distracting loop. Anything shorter
  becomes a 30-second cycle that the player notices.

## Hand-composition tips (your zone, but worth naming)

You compose 12 tracks totaling ~25 minutes. A few constraints worth
respecting:

**Mix headroom.** Master to -6dB peak so we have room for the auto-duck
behavior. AVAudioMixerNode applies attenuation cleanly — don’t try to
build the duck into the master.

**Loop points matter.** Every “seamless” loop track must have a hard
loop point that’s musically inaudible. AVAudioPlayer can do gapless
looping but only if the file’s loop point is sample-accurate. Render
to PCM first, edit the loop point in a DAW, then encode to M4A.

**Stems if you can.** For each hero track, render at minimum a “with
melodic lead” and “without melodic lead” stem. This lets the engine
duck JUST the lead during voice playback without losing the bed —
much more musical than ducking the whole track.

**Tempo continuity.** The tracks at the same emotional level should sit
at compatible BPMs so transitions don’t feel jarring. The CSV recommends
specific BPMs for each track that form a coherent map: swipe (78), chat
(70), date warm (75), date tense (70), journal (80), accusation (95).
Endings vary by emotion.

**Bundle size.** All music files at 192kbps VBR M4A is a good target.
~25 minutes at 192kbps ≈ 35MB total music. Stays well under the Date
Mode PRD bundle budget.

## What’s NOT in the audio sheet (intentionally)

- **Character voices.** Already in Date Mode PRD Epic 2. Voice cast,
  pre-generated lines, voice IDs, pricing — all there.
- **Friend NPC voices.** Same, in PRD Epic 2.1.
- **Narrator voice.** Optional, also PRD Epic 2.1.
- **Per-character musical themes.** Considered and rejected. Five themes
  - bedroom + swipe + chat + journal + accusation + endings = OST bloat.
    The match characters get musical *texture* via their date-scene
    emotional state (warm/tense), not personal leitmotifs.
- **Adaptive music systems.** No FMOD, no horizontal/vertical re-sequencing.
  Crossfades between fixed tracks are sufficient for v1.
- **Spatial audio / 3D positioning.** Mobile target, headphones aren’t
  guaranteed. Stereo throughout.
- **Voiced friend NPC reactions to clues.** Friends are TEXT-only in the
  Corgi Cabal chat per Epic 7.7. Audio is just message_received chimes.

## Open questions

1. **ElevenLabs music API pricing at this volume.** 53 generated clips
   plus iteration runs (3-4 attempts per sting to cherry-pick) means
   probably 200+ generation calls. Need real cost projection before
   batch-running.
1. **License terms for ElevenLabs-generated audio.** Confirm the music
   they generate is commercially redistributable in a paid mobile game.
   Their terms have changed before — verify current state before
   shipping.
1. **Adaptive vs. fixed crossfade timing for warm↔tense date music.**
   Do we crossfade on every Focus Shift (could feel manipulative) or
   only on first tell-eligible choice in a scene (cleaner)? Decide
   before composing the two date loops.
1. **Voice-of-the-game for narrator stings.** Do we want a narrator at
   all, or are the stings enough to mark scene transitions? Lighter
   touch is probably better, but worth deciding before composing
   `main_theme` (which sets the project tone).
1. **Soundtrack release as separate product?** The 12 hand-composed
   tracks could ship as a Bandcamp release at ~$5. Free marketing,
   minimal additional work, validates “this is a real game with a
   real OST.” Worth thinking about now even if the call is later.
